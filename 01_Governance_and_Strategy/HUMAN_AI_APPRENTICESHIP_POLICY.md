# Human–AI Apprenticeship Policy

## Purpose

Use AI to amplify human judgement without eroding the practical, technical, and critical capability required to specify, challenge, validate, recover, and improve work. Automation is not evidence that a person or team understands the result.

## Operating Principles

1. Define the competence a role must retain before delegating a task to AI: domain reasoning, requirements interpretation, method selection, test design, edge-case review, and accountable approval where applicable.
2. Give developing practitioners supervised practice on representative work, including controlled manual or low-automation exercises where that is necessary to understand the work and detect a flawed AI result.
3. Separate AI draft generation from independent human review. Rotate reviewer and author responsibilities where practical so that critical judgement is practised rather than centralised in one person.
4. Turn accepted defects, rejected AI outputs, disputes, and near misses into short reusable learning cases. Keep examples sanitised and within the project data boundary.
5. Permit a role to use AI at a level supported by evidence: assisted learning, supervised operation, or independent operation with the applicable quality gates. Do not infer competence from prompt volume, speed, token use, or tool activity.

## Minimum Evidence for Material Capability

For a material delivery role, maintain proportionate evidence of: a defined competence target, representative review or assessment cases, feedback and correction history, demonstrated ability to explain critical decisions, and an assigned coach or reviewer where the capability is not yet established. This is development evidence, not a surveillance system or an automatic employment decision.

## Operational Job Instruction

For repeatable or safety-relevant work, the role owner may use a short job instruction that records the task and applicability, prerequisites, important steps, key points, reasons, hold points, abnormal-condition response, evidence to retain, and who may approve independent operation. Teach it by preparing the learner, demonstrating the work, supervised practice with explanation, and proportionate follow-up on representative cases.

Use a one-page visual aid, short recording, or decision record only when it improves execution or recovery. Concision is not permission to omit a safety step, source, revision, jurisdictional requirement, rollback route, accessibility need, or competent-person decision. Training duration and review cadence are selected from task risk and observed performance; there is no universal daily ritual, video length, search-time target, or fixed habit-formation period.

## Roles and KPIs

Learning and Transfer Coach owns the learning design; Organizational Psychology and Human Factors Advisor assesses workload and human-AI interaction effects; Quality Manager defines applicable quality evidence; role owners provide domain cases. Track validated learning transfer, review-detection effectiveness, repeat-defect trend, time to independent competent performance, and avoidable rework. Interpret trends at a suitable aggregated level and never use an activity metric alone for an individual decision.

## Boundaries

- Do not mandate manual work merely to preserve tradition when automation is safe and verified.
- Do not automate final responsibility, training sign-off, production approval, or a competence assessment without the applicable human and quality controls.
- Do not place customer production content in learning examples unless the project data-flow record explicitly permits that route.
