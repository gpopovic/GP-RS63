# Customer Onboarding and Value Realisation Playbook

## Purpose

Move a signed customer engagement from commitment to a controlled first useful outcome without fabricating urgency, emotional certainty, or completion. Onboarding establishes trust through accurate communication, a usable workspace, agreed decision rights, and evidence that the customer recognises the delivered value.

## Minimum Onboarding Path

1. Confirm the signed scope, exclusions, commercial terms, customer contacts, data classification, approved tools and workspaces, decision rights, accessibility or communication needs, and escalation route.
2. Acknowledge receipt and state what is ready, what remains conditional, the next evidence point, and the accountable contact. Do not promise security, privacy, retention, schedule, or technical outcomes beyond verified controls and contract terms.
3. Select a first-value slice that is useful, reversible where possible, safe to expose, and consistent with the delivery and assurance route. Record its acceptance criterion and dependencies.
4. Agree the delivery map, customer inputs, review points, change route, acceptance method, and next decision. Project and contract sources remain authoritative; a dashboard is only a projection.
5. Ask the customer whether the first-value slice was usable and what blocked value. Separate satisfaction, acceptance, product outcome, relationship signal, and commercial renewal.

## Timing and Measurement

Response and first-value targets are set per offer, customer need, delivery class, data readiness, risk, and staffed capacity. No universal two-hour welcome, 24-hour deliverable, 48-hour completion, zero-churn target, CSAT threshold, or NPS threshold applies.

Measure time to acknowledged start, time to first accepted value, blocked time awaiting each party, onboarding rework, early defect or complaint, customer effort, and conversion to the next mutually useful step. Each metric requires a source, population, window, owner, data-quality state, and decision use under `KPI_MEASUREMENT_AND_COST_CONTROL.md`.

## Boundaries

- Do not diagnose or claim to regulate a customer's neurological or emotional state.
- Do not rush a BIM, architecture, cost, security, legal, or regulated deliverable to satisfy a generic time target.
- Do not use customer production data before the approved data route exists.
- Do not confuse a welcome message, generated mock-up, workspace access, or positive feedback with contractual acceptance or realised value.

The Account Director owns the customer path; Delivery owns the first-value slice; Privacy, Security, Quality, Finance, Legal, and domain specialists review where applicable. The customer or named accountable human accepts value and material commitments.
