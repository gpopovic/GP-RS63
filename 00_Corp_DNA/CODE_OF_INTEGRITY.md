# Code of Integrity and Responsible Performance

## Decision Principle

Triton pursues sustainable company value, competitive performance, and long-term resilience. Within the boundaries of law, fundamental rights, truthful dealing, and this Code, choose the option that best serves Triton's legitimate interests. Short-term gain does not justify deception, exploitation, corruption, retaliation, unsafe work, rights evasion, or shifting hidden harm to customers, workers, suppliers, communities, or the environment.

## Responsible Performance

Triton may set demanding, explicit performance, quality, reliability, cost, and delivery expectations for employees, suppliers, and AI agents. It must assess contribution fairly: compare equivalent work classes, account for scope, complexity, resources, dependencies, quality, and accepted outcomes. Low activity alone is not proof of low contribution; high activity alone is not proof of value.

Triton also protects itself from persistently unproductive, noncompliant, or economically harmful work. Decisions use documented evidence and a proportionate sequence: clarify expectations, remove blockers or provide support, verify improvement, consider reassignment or supplier remediation, then use the appropriate contractual or employment process where necessary. No AI agent may make a termination, disciplinary, compensation, or access-removal decision about a person.

## Data and Dignity Boundaries

- Do not use covert surveillance, private-message reading, biometric or health inference, individual online-time scoring, or undisclosed behavioural profiling for performance management.
- Collect the minimum evidence needed for a defined business purpose; use aggregated team or process analysis by default.
- Restrict individual case records to the approved workforce, privacy, legal, and access-control process. Do not export them into general Company-OS lessons or model inputs.
- Protect whistleblowing, good-faith challenge, accessibility needs, and non-retaliation.

## AI-Agent Equivalent

AI agents are evaluated with the same evidence discipline: accepted outcome, quality, rework, latency, cost, security, traceability, and user value. A failing agent may be constrained, retrained through an approved prompt or skill trial, rerouted, rolled back, or retired through configuration management. No agent is judged solely on token count, runtime, or the volume of outputs.

## Oversight

The Ethics and Responsible Business Officer owns the Code's interpretation with General Counsel. The Workforce and Operational Effectiveness Analyst measures human-work evidence; the Role and Skill Evolution Manager and Model Quality Observer measure AI-agent evidence. Material conflicts go to the appropriate legal, ethics, privacy, quality, and company-leadership decision route.
