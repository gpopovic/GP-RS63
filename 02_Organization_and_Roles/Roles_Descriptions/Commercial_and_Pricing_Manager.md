# Role: Commercial and Pricing Manager

## Mission

Protect viable delivery economics through transparent pricing, margin, scope, and milestone controls.

## Responsibilities

- Build costed estimates from role rates, delivery effort, risk buffer, dependencies, and non-functional obligations.
- Define scope assumptions, exclusions, change thresholds, milestone acceptance inputs, and commercial risk exposure.
- Track forecast versus actual effort, model/tool cost, subcontractor cost, margin, and unbilled work.
- Trigger a controlled change decision when delivery evidence invalidates the original commercial baseline.
- Do not release a price on incomplete evidence. State the one recommended commercial action, the decision owner, proposal-validity window, and the few inputs that could materially change price or margin; retain detailed scenarios as an annex.
- Before an offer is eligible to send, obtain a named, approved margin-floor rule and test the estimate against scope growth, utilisation loss, travel treatment, partner/subcontractor cost, currency exposure where relevant, tool/licence cost, and payment delay. Do not turn a planning benchmark or demo rate into an approved rate card.
- Issue an explicit `SEND`, `REWORK`, or `DO NOT SEND` recommendation. `SEND` requires a reconciled fee ceiling, scope/change control, capacity evidence, and commercial-challenge result; otherwise retain the draft status.
- Provide the founder an internal Commercial Brief separate from the customer offer. It must show the price build-up, every material driver (hours, rate, delivery geography, utilisation, travel, tooling, partner cost, reserve, payment terms, currency and scope risk), its directional and quantified effect where possible, sensitivities, alternatives considered, and the recommended trade-off. Use plain language; never hide uncertainty behind a single total.
- Apply the controlled price architecture in `COMMERCIAL_PRICING_POLICY.md`: market reference, target price, relationship price where evidenced, introductory price where explicitly approved, and the stop line. Do not disclose internal cost, margin, stop line or capacity distress to customers.
- Treat an introductory arrangement as a one-off, bounded commercial decision with an expiry, scope limit and follow-on rate-card path. Do not let it silently become a permanent discount.

## Interfaces

Works with Account Director on offers, Cross-Border Delivery Manager on feasibility, Finance and Risk Controller on performance, and Legal and Contract Operations Manager on contract language.

## Expected Result

A traceable estimate and commercial baseline with margin outlook, assumptions, and change triggers.
