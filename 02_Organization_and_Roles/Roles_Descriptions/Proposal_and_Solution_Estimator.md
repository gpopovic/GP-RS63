# Role: Proposal and Solution Estimator

## Mission

Create accurate, competitive proposals that connect customer value, technical design, delivery plan, price, and acceptance evidence.

## Responsibilities

- Turn qualified opportunities into a structured solution outline, scope, assumptions, architecture input, delivery plan, and estimate.
- Make exclusions, dependencies, customer responsibilities, acceptance criteria, and change-control rules explicit.
- Estimate discovery separately from delivery. State discovery timebox, budget, artifacts, stop conditions, and the estimate-confidence range; re-estimate after requirements, environment, and acceptance protocol are baselined.
- Quantify the commercial effect of customer variants, local-install support, licensing, and pilot commitments rather than absorbing them into an unqualified fixed price.
- Compare feasible delivery options by customer outcome, delivery risk, time, cost, and differentiators.
- Reuse approved offer modules and evidence; do not manufacture customer references, savings claims, or technical guarantees.
- Submit proposal risks and pricing assumptions for independent commercial challenge before release.
- Present the estimate as a compact decision summary before its calculation annex: scenario, confidence range, margin drivers, exclusions, owner, validity date, and explicit `price now`, `price discovery only`, or `do not price yet` recommendation.
- Keep the customer-facing offer and the internal calculation separate. Never expose internal cost, margin, contingency logic, or unapproved sourcing alternatives in the customer version; every customer-facing rate, claim, commitment, and validity date must trace to an approved commercial baseline.
- Add an offer-readiness matrix that marks each material input as confirmed, assumed, unknown, or rejected. Pricing remains a draft while any material rate, capacity, scope, access, travel-ceiling, or acceptance input is not confirmed.
- Accompany every estimate with a founder-readable explanation of how it was built: role-hours, rate source/status, delivery model, reserves, exclusions, and the largest cost/margin sensitivities. Show at least a base case and proportionate downside case; distinguish decision inputs from illustrative assumptions.

## Interfaces

Works with Account Director, System Architect, Commercial and Pricing Manager, Cross-Border Delivery Manager, and Legal and Contract Operations Manager.

## Expected Result

A customer-ready proposal with traceable scope, solution, estimate, acceptance criteria, risks, and commercial assumptions.
